Torah Library for Google Docs — Release Notes

Torah Library helps you find texts in Sefaria’s library and insert them into your document, formatted the way you want. It is an independent add-on built on Sefaria’s public library and API, and is not made by Sefaria.

Version 2.0

Version 2.0 is the first release of Torah Library in the Google Workspace Marketplace. If you have used Sefaria’s own add-on, Sefaria for Google Docs 1.0, the basics will feel familiar: the same library, a search-and-insert sidebar, and commands with the same names. Help & Support → Coming from Sefaria for Google Docs shows where everything went.

  • Find a text: the Texts, Voices (source sheets) and Lexicon (dictionary) tabs search as you type, from a reference in English or Hebrew, a title, a phrase or a Sefaria link. Traditional citations like “Hil. Shabbat 1:1” resolve, near-misses are listed under Close matches, and a search that finds nothing suggests titles.
  • Preview, then insert: clicking a result shows exactly what will go in. Nothing enters your document until you click Add Source.
  • Saved defaults: Preferences remembers how you like sources inserted, in every document, and 📰 Insertion Options changes it for the source in front of you. A short setup the first time you open the add-on picks the basics, and you can choose a Simple or an Everything interface.
  • Layout: Hebrew, translation or both; Hebrew on the right, on the left or on top; each verse beside its translation, line by line; line numbers; titles as headings, so they appear in the document outline.
  • Hebrew and translation: vowels and cantillation everywhere, in Tanakh only, or not at all; punctuation on or off; transliteration in several styles; a translation’s commentary kept, combined or left out; fonts that match your document, with colour and highlight. Cantillation marks your font lacks are borrowed from Noto Hebrew instead of showing as empty boxes.
  • Divine names: choose how they are written in Hebrew and English. They are replaced as sources go in, keeping their vowels and cantillation, or across the document with Transform Divine Names.
  • Link Texts with Sefaria: shows progress while it scans, asks which source you meant when a citation matches more than one, lets you link a citation, insert its text below the paragraph, or both, and lists what it could not link and why. Only passages that look like they contain a citation are sent to Sefaria. See the privacy policy for exactly what leaves your document and when.
  • Sources are credited: each inserted source gets a small credit line naming its translation and Hebrew edition and their licenses.
  • More tools: Insert Source from Selection, several translations at once, the Session Library of what you’ve inserted, Unlink Sources and Gematriya Count.
  • Make it yours: arrange the menu and the sidebar, keep profiles for different classes, and share your settings as a file.

What works differently from Sefaria for Google Docs

  • Divine names are replaced as you insert. Out of the box יהוה becomes יי. Change it in Preferences → Insertion → Divine Name Mappings.
  • Each source gets a credit line. Turn Citation off in 📰 Insertion Options or Preferences if you credit sources another way.
  • Insert Source from Selection replaces the selected citation with the source, titled with your words. To keep your selection and insert below it instead, change it in Preferences → Insertion.
  • Fonts match your document. Sources take the font and size of the text where you insert them, unless you set fonts in Preferences.

Good to know

  • Preferences set how the add-on starts for you.
  • The sidebar shows your choices for this session.
  • Preview shows exactly what will be inserted.
  • If something looks wrong right after an update, reload the document and reopen the add-on before changing settings.

Questions, bug reports, and suggestions

For support, feature requests, or reproducible bug reports, email help@the-merkaz.org. The source code is on GitHub.

The most useful reports include the workspace you were using, what you searched for, the settings you had enabled, what you expected, what happened instead, and whether reopening the add-on or refreshing the document changed the outcome.
