Quick start: insert your first source

  1. Put your cursor where the source should go.
  2. Open Extensions → Sefaria for Google Docs → Texts. The sidebar opens on the right.
  3. Type a reference such as Genesis 1:1, Berakhot 2a or בראשית א:א. Results appear as you type.
  4. Click a result. The Preview shows the selected passage with your current text choices.
  5. Optional: open 📰 Insertion Options to choose Hebrew, translation or both, and how they sit on the page.
  6. Click Add Source (or press Ctrl/⌘ + Enter).

To change your initial choices, use Re-run Setup Wizard at the top of this window.

Used Sefaria for Google Docs?

Where everything went, and what's different.

Coming from version 1.0

If you used version 1.0 of Sefaria for Google Docs, the basics will feel familiar: the same Sefaria library, the same search-and-insert sidebar, and commands with the same names. The update changes nothing in your documents. Version 1.0 kept no settings, so version 2.0 starts from its own defaults, and a short setup picks the basics.

What stays the same

  • Hebrew and translation together go in as a two-column table with the Hebrew on the right, as before.
  • Links and text version 1.0 put in your documents stay as they are. Transform Divine Names, Link Texts and Unlink Sources work on them like any other text.

What's new

  • Saved defaults. Preferences remembers how you like sources inserted, in every document. A short setup the first time you open the add-on picks the basics — choose I've used Sefaria for Google Docs 1.0 to apply familiar active settings immediately, including a Texts-only sidebar; Re-run Setup Wizard at the top brings it back; keep or reset your active preferences first.
  • Layout and formatting. Hebrew on the right, on the left or on top, or line by line beside its translation; vowels and cantillation (all, Tanakh only, or none); punctuation; transliteration; fonts that match your document; titles as headings, so they appear in the outline. See Insertion Options.
  • Commentary and notes. Keep, combine or leave out inline explanation in supported Talmud and Tanakh editions; choose separately how source footnotes appear. See Insertion Options.
  • Insert Source from Selection, the Voices (source sheets) and Lexicon (dictionary) tabs, the Session Library of what you've inserted, and several translations at once.
  • Settings files, in Preferences → Import/Export (⇅), so a teacher can hand a class the same settings, and profiles, so you can keep one look for each class and switch in a click.

What works differently

  1. Divine names are replaced as you insert. Out of the box יהוה becomes יי in every new source, so you don't have to run Transform Divine Names afterwards. Choose the spelling, or turn this off, in the setup or in Preferences → Insertion → Divine Name Mappings. Transform Divine Names asks before it changes anything, and works on the tab you have open.
  2. Link Texts tells you what it did. It links the citations it is sure of, then shows a summary, asks when a citation could mean more than one text, and lists what it couldn't link. Tick Insert on a row to put the source's text in as well. It sends Sefaria only the passages that look like citations. Preferences → Linking can review every match before linking, just link and report a count, or send the whole document.
  3. A credit line under each source names the translation and Hebrew edition and their licenses, which many Sefaria texts require. Turn Citation off in 📰 Insertion Options or Preferences if you credit sources another way.
  4. Clicking a result only previews it. Nothing goes into your document until you click Add Source.
  5. It asks for less. Version 2.0 does not ask to see your email address or profile; it needs only this document, its own sidebar and dialogs, its settings, and Sefaria.

More help is in Sefaria's Help Center, at help.sefaria.org.

Find a text (Texts tab)

Type into the search box. The search runs as you type (after two characters), or press Enter. You can search for:

  • a reference — Exodus 20:1-14, Berakhot 2a, Mishnah Avot 1:1
  • a reference in Hebrew — שמות כ:א, ברכות ב.
  • a traditional citation — Hil. Shabbat 1:1, Rambam, Hil. Teshuvah 3:4
  • a book title, a phrase from the text, or a link copied from sefaria.org

If nothing matches, the sidebar suggests related titles instead of leaving the list empty. Click one to search for it.

While the search box is empty, the Texts tab lists your most recent and pinned sources — click one to open it again.

A book Sefaria opens at its first chapter — Genesis on its own — is different: the preview shows that chapter, titled Genesis 1, and Add Source inserts that one chapter, not the whole book (for a one-chapter book such as Obadiah, that chapter is the whole book). Check the title in the preview, and add a chapter or verse if you meant another passage.

Versions & Preview

Under the selected source, Versions lets you choose which translation to insert (Translation Versions) and, when you need a specific edition, which Hebrew text (Original Versions). Your preferred translation language is set in Preferences → Insertion → Insertion Defaults → Translation → Language; if it isn't available the sidebar tries English, then any other language.

Changing Insertion Options keeps the editions you selected. A refresh retains the translation, the Hebrew edition and the order of several selected translations. Add Source waits while the updated passage loads, so the version names and text agree.

Several translations at once

When the Display is Translation only, or Hebrew and translation in the Stacked layout, click a translation to select just that one; hold Ctrl (or ⌘ on Mac) and click to add or remove one, or hold Shift and click to select a visible range. Hold both modifier keys to extend the selection. Each is inserted as its own block, in selection order, with a blank line between. Newly selected translations in a range are added in list order. The Preview can't show several translations at once, so it lists the ones selected, in that order. One translation always stays selected: trying to remove the last one leaves it selected and says so. Typing in the filter box only narrows the list; a translation it hides stays selected until you replace the selection with a plain or Shift-click.

Finding a language

The filter box above Translation Versions finds a language by its name, in English, in the language itself, or in any of the other languages in Preferences → Search → Translation Languages: French, français, צרפתית and Französisch all list the French translations. The first few letters are enough, and accents, vowel points and capitals don't matter (francais works). The language's code works too (fr, de), and typing part of a version's title still finds it.

The list reflects the editions Sefaria returns for this passage, so it can differ between verses. A catalogued translation with no text here is left out. Preferences → Search → Advanced → Show unavailable translations shows known unavailable entries greyed out; it cannot show an edition Sefaria omitted from this passage's catalog. To leave whole languages out, untick them in Preferences → Search → Translation Languages.

A partial edition keeps its gaps. An edition may translate only some verses in a range. The add-on does not fill those gaps from another edition under the same credit. Choose another edition if you need more of the passage. If a selected edition needed for your Display is unavailable, the pickers stay visible and Add Source waits until you make a valid choice.

Preview

🔁 Preview shows what Add Source will insert right now: the current version, display, layout, vowels, cantillation, transliteration, Commentary, Notes and divine-name replacements. With one translation selected, the preview and insertion use the same rules for which words and notes appear.

The preview is a guide to your text choices. Check generated line numbers, chapter headings, final fonts and page breaks in the document. With several translations selected, it lists their names instead of rendering every translation.

Source bold, italics, superscripts and subscripts are supported. An image's text description is kept when Sefaria supplies one; the image itself and arbitrary web-page styling are not inserted.

Insertion Options (📰)

Click 📰 Insertion Options under Add Source. Everything here applies to this sidebar session only — your saved Preferences don't change unless you click 💾 Save as defaults, at the top of the panel. The panel scrolls; Esc or a click outside it closes it.

Revert, Save as defaults and profile changes wait for any setting still saving. If a setting cannot be saved after a retry, insertion pauses and the sidebar keeps your choices visible. Change an option to retry, or choose Revert to restore saved defaults.

The same controls appear in two other places: Preferences → Insertion, where they set your saved defaults, and Link Texts with Sefaria's 📰 Customize this insertion panel, where they apply to the sources inserted in one pass.

With two or more profiles, the button names the one in use and the panel's Profile list switches between them. In Everything, Preferences → Sidebar can leave controls out of this panel; hiding a control leaves its current value in force, including an existing session override. Use Revert to return session choices to saved defaults.

Start with the controls at the top. Display, Arrangement, Line by line and Transliteration are within reach without opening a group, when they apply to the selected display. Text and notes, References and numbering, and Fonts and appearance start folded; their summaries show the current choices. Commentary: Default keeps explanation as the edition marks it; Notes: Omit leaves out marked footnotes. Simple and Everything use the same panel. Preferences → Sidebar can hide controls you never change.

If the chosen Display language has no text, choose the other language or Both; Both uses the language that is available. If Commentary or Notes leaves no content, insertion stops with an explanation before changing the document. With several translations, an edition emptied by those choices is omitted with a notice and remains selectable for different options.

The controls at the top

Display has text labels beside its icons: Translation (A), Both (A + א), and Original (א). With Both selected, Arrangement offers Stacked (Hebrew above the translation), Hebrew right or Hebrew left (side by side).

Line by line stays beside those choices whenever both languages are included. Side by side, each verse or line gets its own table row beside its translation. Stacked, each line is followed by its translation, or by each selected translation in the order picked. With Line by line off, the whole passage sits in one row, or Stacked in one block per language. The Arrangement icons sketch those choices and the transliteration's position.

Transliteration also stays at the top whenever the original text is included. Style appears when it is on; Position appears for side-by-side bilingual layouts. Neither switch requires opening a group. Controls you explicitly hide in Preferences → Sidebar remain hidden.

Open a group to change its settings; closing it changes no value and does not reload the source. Closed summaries show Notes and Commentary choices, vowel and cantillation choices, credits and title links, line numbers, and selected fonts and sizes. Transliteration

Add a transliteration of the Hebrew. While it is on, Style under it picks the scheme — Simple English, Traditional, Modern Israeli, Ashkenazi, Academic, ALA-LC (Library of Congress), BGN/PCGN 2018, or IPA — and, side by side, Position where it goes: Below the Hebrew, in its column, or a column of its own Left or Right of the Hebrew, headed by the Hebrew title in Latin letters, linked like the Hebrew title: Sefaria's own Latin name for it, with the chapter and verse in digits (Bereshit 1:1-2, Shmuel Aleph 1:1, Berakhot 2a:1; with the Ashkenazi style, Bereishis and Berachos). Where Sefaria has none, the Hebrew title is transliterated in your style, or else shown as Sefaria's Latin reference (Rashi on Genesis 1:1:1). Stacked always puts it under the Hebrew, with several translations too; with Line by line each line's transliteration goes with that line, under it or in its row. It is read from the fully vowelled text Sefaria provides, so it is complete even with Vowels off; with the Hebrew’s Punctuation off it leaves out the same punctuation, and a maqaf becomes a space. Each sentence starts with a capital letter (after a sof pasuq or a period), and so does each name of a person, place or people in the Tanakh and the Talmud — Mosheh, miMitzrayim, Beit-El, Rabbi Akiva — except in IPA. A name that is also an ordinary word (מאיר, אשר) is capitalized only where it is plainly the name: by its vowels, or after רבי, רב or בן. The Name — יהוה and every substitute for it (יי, יקוק, ה', השם) — is written HaShem, or as you set it in Preferences → Insertion → Transliteration Mapping → Write the Name as (Adonai, Adonoy, G-d or your own), in every style, with a prefix kept: לַיהוָה is laHaShem or laAdonai. Example: the Talmud without the explanation

The William Davidson Edition - English of the Babylonian Talmud includes Rabbi Steinsaltz's translation and explanation. It prints the Talmud's own words in bold and the explanation in plain text. With Commentary set to Combine, it all reads as one text; set to Drop, the bold translation remains. This illustrates the convention:

Fonts and cantillation marks

The Text font and Title font buttons pick the fonts. Many Hebrew fonts in Google Docs (David Libre, Frank Ruhl Libre, Heebo and others) have the letters and vowels but no cantillation marks, which Docs would show as empty boxes. So the add-on sets just the marks a font lacks in Noto Serif Hebrew (Noto Sans Hebrew for a sans-serif font); the letters, and every mark the font has, stay in the font you chose. The preview shows the text the same way.

Inserting a source

Add Source inserts at your cursor in the document. The insertion is, in order:

  1. a title (the reference) — linked to Sefaria if Link title to Sefaria is on (the source and translation titles each have their own);
  2. the Hebrew, transliteration and/or translation, arranged by your Display and Arrangement;
  3. a citation of the translation and Hebrew edition, if Citation is on, styled as set in Preferences → Insertion → Layout → Citation style.

Fonts, sizes and colours come from Preferences → Insertion: click any font button there (Title font, Text font, the transliteration's Font, Layout's Citation style) to open its settings in place. By default each is Match the document: inserted text takes the font and size of the text where you're inserting, so it blends in. The sidebar labels this choice Match formatting. You can make titles a heading (Heading 1–6, Layout's Title style) so they appear in the document outline and table of contents.

In a table: with the cursor in a table cell, one language or the Stacked layout goes inside the cell. Hebrew right and Hebrew left are tables themselves, so they go directly below the table, and the sidebar tells you so. Sources can't go into a header, footer or footnote.

Sefaria's own bold and italics are kept by default. Commentary changes recognized inline explanation in the William Davidson English Talmud and Silverstein's Rashi Chumash and Rashi Ketuvim; other editions keep their text and source emphasis. Notes independently omits marked footnotes, includes them inline, or collects them below each passage. See Insertion Options for the edition conventions and examples. You can change how retained emphasis looks under Preferences → Insertion → Insertion Defaults → Commentary → Advanced.

Transliteration styles

The transliteration reads each word the way Hebrew is read, before it writes a letter:

  • a vowel letter is part of its vowel, not a consonant: אֱלֹהֵינוּ Eloheinu, טוֹב tov, בְּרֵאשִׁית Bereshit;
  • a sh'va is spoken at the start of a word, under a doubled letter, after another sh'va or a long vowel (Shema, kideshanu), and silent where it closes a syllable (Yisra'el, mitzvot);
  • a kamatz katan is "o": kol, chokhmah. Sefaria's Tanakh marks every one; elsewhere the transliteration recognizes it by its closed, unstressed syllable, which a few words share with a kamatz gadol;
  • a patach under a final ח or ע is read first: ruach, mizbeach;
  • a dagesh chazak doubles its letter only with Double dagesh chazak on (Preferences → Insertion → Transliteration Mapping): Shabbat, tefillah; off, Shabat, tefilah. Two-letter sounds (sh, ts, kh, ch) are written once either way.

Voices (source sheets)

  1. Open the Voices tab (or Extensions → Sefaria for Google Docs → Voices). No Voices tab? Switch it on in Preferences → Sidebar.
  2. Search Sefaria's source sheets by topic, title or text. The sheets are grouped by topic (Torah Portions, Shabbat & Holidays, Prayer, Tanakh, People…; a sheet goes under the first of its topics that fits one), and the topic chips over the list work like corpora in Texts: click one, or a group's ❌, to hide it for this search, and again to bring it back. To change the order of the groups, or hide one for good, use Preferences → Search → Voices Topics.
  3. Pick a sheet. Under What to insert choose Citation (just the reference), Text (the sheet's text) or Both (the text, its title linked to the sheet on Sefaria), and optionally a Transliteration scheme. A sample shows how it will look, and the line underneath says what will be inserted.
  4. Click Add Sheet.

With a transliteration chosen, each Hebrew passage of the sheet's text is followed by its transliteration in parentheses — הַסֵּפֶר כָל־הָעָם (Hasefer khol-ha'am) — so it stays with the Hebrew it reads. Only Hebrew is transliterated: the sheet's English, comments and media are inserted as they are. Citation alone inserts no text, so it has nothing to transliterate. The Voices transliteration is its own setting; it never changes the one for Texts.

Voices starts with Citation and transliteration None. Your usual choices can be saved in Preferences → Insertion → Insertion Defaults → Voices.

Lexicon (dictionary)

  1. Open the Lexicon tab and type a Hebrew or Aramaic word, or an English one to find the Hebrew words that mean it. The ⌨︎ keyboard helps if you don't have Hebrew set up. No Lexicon tab? Switch it on in Preferences → Sidebar.
  2. The results are words. Matches are spelled as you typed; Related words are the ones it's a form of or close to (for שם: שׂוּם, put, and שׁום, assess). Each word shows which dictionaries have it and what they say it means. The dictionary chips over the list work like corpora in Texts: click one to hide its entries for this search, and again to bring them back. To change the order dictionaries are listed in, or hide one for good, use Preferences → Search → Lexicon Dictionaries.
  3. Pick a word. Its entries are listed: one per dictionary, and one per meaning where a dictionary has two words spelled alike (BDB's שֵׁם I, name, and II, Shem).   4. Under What to insert choose Full entry (the default: the word, each dictionary's name, its numbered definitions and notes), Word + ref (the word and the dictionaries' names), or Definition (the definitions alone).
  5. The preview shows the picked entries as an outline, each definition cut to two lines; Show the whole entry opens a long one. Click Add Entry.

Texts, Voices and Lexicon tabs

  • Switching tabs never searches. Each tab keeps its results, its hidden corpora, topics or dictionaries, and what you had selected; the search box keeps its text. A tab with no results for that text shows Search Voices for “…” — click it to search.

Session Library (📜)

Every source, sheet or word you insert is added to the Session Library (the most recent twelve, plus up to thirty you pin), so you can find it again. Open it with 📜 at the bottom of the sidebar, or Ctrl/⌘ + Shift + L.

  • Click one to open it again, in its own tab (Texts, Voices or Lexicon), selected and ready to insert.
  • 📌 pins it so it stays at the top and isn't cleared. Every result has a 📌: a text, a source sheet, a word.
  • Filter by Pinned, Recent or a category; search; and sort by category, date added, or A–Z. ⊟ switches to a compact list.

Link Texts with Sefaria

Turns the citations already in your document — Genesis 1:1, Berakhot 2a, Rambam, Hil. Teshuvah 3:4 — into links to Sefaria.

  1. Run 🔗 → ס from Quick Actions, or Extensions → Sefaria for Google Docs → Quick Actions → Link Texts with Sefaria.
  2. A dialog shows progress while your document is scanned.
  3. You get a table of what was found. Each row shows the words in your document and the Sefaria source they'll point to. When a citation could mean more than one source, pick the right one from the dropdown — each option shows a short excerpt to help.
  4. Each row has two checkboxes: Link (hyperlink the words) and Insert (insert that source's text — see Where an inserted source goes below). Tick either, both, or neither. The checkbox in a column header sets the whole column.

Where an inserted source goes

A citation on a line of its own — Genesis 1:1, or (Genesis 1:1). — is replaced by the source, titled with the citation as you wrote it and still linked to Sefaria, the same as Insert Source from Selection. A citation inside a sentence stays where it is, and the source goes below its paragraph. Customize this insertion

Repeated citations are separate occurrences, even when they point to the same source. Previously linked text is left alone. If the resolver misses an exact repetition, the add-on can recover a fully specified citation after a standalone reference check; it does not copy ambiguous or context-dependent matches.

Click 📰 Customize this insertion under the review table. Choosing how much it asks

In Preferences → Linking → After linking:

  • Show a summary, and ask about ambiguous citations (recommended) — links the clear matches and only asks about the unclear ones.
  • Show every match for review — lists everything before anything is linked.
  • Just link and report a count — never asks; unclear citations are skipped rather than guessed. Turn on Insert text after linking to insert each linked source as well.

What is sent to Sefaria

Sefaria's linker has to read text to find citations. Highlight text to limit the scope; clear the selection to scan the whole body of the current document tab. With Candidate passages only (the default, in Preferences → Linking → Document scanning), the add-on filters that scope before sending candidate passages to Sefaria. Whole document sends the full selected scope, or the entire body if nothing is selected. Insert Source from Selection explicitly sends the selected text and always opens review.

Citations that couldn't be matched

When Sefaria's linker can't match a citation, the add-on looks it up again the way the search box does: with consistent punctuation, Hebrew numerals without their marks, and abbreviations such as Hil. written out. A citation found this way is linked like any other, and its row says Found by searching the spelling that worked. When Sefaria takes a label in front of a colon as part of the citation — Duplicate A: Genesis 2:1 — the words after the label are searched too, and only they are linked; the label stays as you wrote it.

The rest are listed under ▸ Show … citations that couldn't be matched, below the table. Each row shows the words in your document, every spelling that was searched with what Sefaria answered to it, and why none matched — usually a chapter or page the book doesn't have, a book name Sefaria doesn't know, or ibid. / there / שם, which points back to an earlier citation. Fix the citation in your document and run Link Texts again.

If the report says some citations fell across a gap in the partial scan, switch Document scanning to Whole document and run it again.

Divine names

The add-on can replace names of God as text is inserted — by default, יהוה becomes יי. The rules are in Preferences → Insertion → Divine Name Mappings:

  • Apply Divine-Name Replacements On Insertion turns replacement on or off for new insertions.
  • Each rule has its own switch and a replacement to choose — יהוה (יי, ה', יקוק, השם), יה, אלוהים, and English God (Adonai, HaShem, G-d) — or Other to type your own.
  • The replacement keeps the vowels and cantillation of the name it replaces: in a pointed verse יְהוָ֥ה becomes יְיָ֥ (or יְקוָ֥ק), and אֱלֹהִ֑ים becomes אֱלוֹקִ֑ים. A replacement that is a different word, such as השם or ה', keeps just the cantillation. One you type with its own vowels is used as typed.

These rules change the Hebrew. The transliteration writes the Name its own way, HaShem unless you choose another (Adonai, Adonoy, G-d or your own) under Preferences → Insertion → Transliteration Mapping → Write the Name as, whichever replacement the Hebrew shows.

To apply the same rules to text that's already in the document, run ה → ש Transform Divine Names from Quick Actions. It changes every match in the body of the tab you have open: selecting text does not limit it, and headers, footers, footnotes and other tabs are left alone. It lists the rules that are on and asks before changing anything, then says how many names it replaced; Edit → Undo reverses it. It runs whether or not replacement on insertion is switched on. If no rules are turned on, it tells you so and changes nothing.

Troubleshooting

My search finds nothing, or the wrong book

Try the reference the way Sefaria writes it (Mishneh Torah, Repentance 3:4), or click one of the suggested titles. Check that the A א language filter isn't set to a language the work lacks, and that you haven't hidden its group with ❌ — ⤦ restores hidden groups.

I switched tabs and it didn't search

That's on purpose: switching between Texts, Voices and Lexicon never searches, so each tab keeps its own results and what you had selected. Press Enter (or 🔍), or click the tab's Search … for “…” button (Search Voices for “shabbat”). Enter also searches the other tabs in the background, so their results are waiting when you switch. A reference such as Genesis 1:1 isn't looked up in the Lexicon. See Texts, Voices and Lexicon tabs.

A translation I want isn't in the Versions list

A translation with no text for this passage is left out; some cover only part of a book. Preferences → Search → Advanced → Show unavailable translations shows known unavailable entries greyed out, but Sefaria may omit an edition from this passage's catalog entirely. A whole language missing? Check that it's ticked in Preferences → Search → Translation Languages. Type a language name or code in the filter box to find one in a long list. See Versions & Preview.

Add Source is greyed out

Read the notice above the preview. Select a result if none is selected; wait if it says Updating preview…; choose another edition in Versions if a required edition is unavailable. A heading with no text needs a specific chapter, verse or daf. A network failure requires retrying when Sefaria can be reached.

The Preview lists several translations instead of showing their text

A multi-translation preview lists the selected names and order. Select just one to see its text, or insert the available selections in that order. An unavailable-edition notice is different: choose an available edition needed for your Display before inserting.

A verse or some explanation is missing, or Drop leaves words in

A partial edition keeps its gaps; choose a fuller edition in Versions. Commentary filters only the documented Davidson and Silverstein conventions. Other editions and malformed bracketed verses keep their words. Notes is separate: choose Inline or Separate to include marked footnotes, or Omit to leave them out. See Commentary and Notes.

The inserted text doesn't look like the Preview, or like my Preferences

The sidebar uses this session's 📰 Insertion Options choices, which can differ from your saved Preferences. Click ↺ Revert (in Insertion Options) to go back to your Preferences, or 💾 Save as defaults to keep what you have.

The Preview still shows the old text after I changed a setting

Vowels, Cantillation, Punctuation and Transliteration are applied when the passage is fetched, so the sidebar fetches it again; the Preview says Updating preview… meanwhile. Give it a few seconds. Add Source waits for it, so what you insert is what you chose.

A tab, a button or a Preferences tab is missing

No Voices or Lexicon tab, Quick Actions button or Insertion Options control? It is switched off in Preferences → Sidebar. Anything else is probably Simple mode, which hides the result sort, Restore Corpus, some menu items, and the Search, Linking and Experimental tabs of Preferences. Switch to Everything at the top of Preferences.

Cantillation marks look different from the rest of the Hebrew

Your Hebrew font has no cantillation marks, so the add-on sets just those marks in Noto Serif Hebrew (or Noto Sans Hebrew). See Fonts and cantillation marks. To keep every mark in one font, pick a font that has them all, such as Noto Serif Hebrew.

The font or size is wrong

With Match the document, inserted text copies the text at your cursor — so inserting inside a heading gives heading-sized text. Put the cursor in an ordinary paragraph, or pick a specific font and size with the font buttons in Preferences → Insertion.

A name of God was replaced and I didn't want it to be

Turn off Apply Divine-Name Replacements On Insertion, or the individual rule, in Preferences → Insertion → Divine Name Mappings. Use Edit → Undo in Google Docs to reverse a recent insertion.

Link Texts with Sefaria missed some citations

Read the report at the end. Could not be placed — set Preferences → Linking → Document scanning to Whole document and try again. Could not be resolved — Sefaria didn't recognise the citation; writing it in a standard form usually helps. In Just link and report a count mode, ambiguous citations are skipped on purpose.

Link Texts replaced my citation with the source

That's the default for a citation on a line of its own: the inserted source is titled with the same words, so nothing is lost and the reference isn't repeated. Use Edit → Undo to reverse it. To keep citations, turn off Preferences → Insertion → Insert from Selection, or untick Replace a citation on its own line in 📰 Customize this insertion for one pass. A citation inside a sentence is never removed.

My menu changes didn't appear

Reload the document. The menu is built when the document opens.

Surprise Me is missing

Quick fixes

  1. Close and reopen the sidebar from Extensions → Sefaria for Google Docs. This clears anything stuck in the sidebar.
  2. Reload the document (your browser's refresh button). Needed after an update or after changing the menu.
  3. Revert to your defaults — 📰 Insertion Options → ↺ Revert — if the output doesn't match your Preferences.
  4. Check the Preview before inserting. The selected words, commentary and notes should agree with the document. Generated line numbers, document fonts and page breaks are checked after insertion.

Reporting a problem

Write to Sefaria through the Help Center, or use Send feedback. These details help us fix it quickly:

  • whether you use Simple or Everything, and where it happened — Texts, Voices, Lexicon, Preferences, Link Texts with Sefaria, or another Quick Action;
  • the reference and exact translation and Hebrew edition names selected in Versions (for example, Berakhot 2a, William Davidson Edition - English);
  • your 📰 Insertion Options settings — Display, Arrangement, Commentary, Notes, and what the three group summaries say is set;
  • what you expected, and what happened instead;
  • whether reopening the sidebar or reloading the document changed anything;
  • the add-on version, shown on the About tab.
A screenshot of the sidebar and the document is the single most useful thing you can send. Please don't send private document text you'd rather we didn't see — a short sample that shows the problem is enough.

Your privacy

Your searches and text go only to sefaria.org, and only when you search, insert a source, or run Link Texts with Sefaria. (The add-on’s windows also load a script library, a stylesheet and a font from Google, and images from Sefaria, like any web page; those requests carry none of your data.) Your preferences and profiles are stored with your Google account; your Session Library (with your pinned texts, sheets and words) and recent searches stay in your own browser, except that the Session Library's list passes briefly through Google when you open and close its window. The add-on has no account or server of its own and sends your text to no one else. The privacy policy has the details.
